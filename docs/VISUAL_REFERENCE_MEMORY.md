## User feedback

The current viewport quality is unacceptable to the user. Prioritize finished
character art and coherent presentation over increasing feature/slider counts.
The existing surface-shell clothing, bare head, coarse garment boundaries,
uniform materials, and unposed character are a technical preview, not the target.

## Non-explicit visual qualities to retain

- Stylized 3D character art with clean, deliberate silhouettes and smooth forms.
- Expressive faces: intentional eye shapes, brows, lashes, lips, and readable
  expression rather than a blank mannequin face.
- Long, flowing hair with a sculpted main shape, layered locks, directional
  highlights, and a consistent hairline.
- Clothing constructed as garments: thickness, seams, folds, trim, and material
  separation. Fabric must not look like body paint or a shiny duplicate skin.
- Coherent material response: soft skin highlights, controlled fabric roughness,
  hair sheen, and limited shiny accents. Avoid an all-over plastic appearance.
- Warm cinematic key light with softer fill, a separating rim light, convincing
  contact shadows, and balanced exposure.
- Intentional staging, camera framing, and environment detail appropriate to
  a finished character portrait. The interface must help inspect the actual model.
- Detailed body customization remains a priority, including breast and glute
  proportion controls, with smooth shape transitions and garment fit checks.

## Scope of use

Use these art qualities for original, clearly adult, non-explicit characters in
ordinary clothing and neutral presentation. Do not reproduce explicit sexual
actions or imagery from the references. The analysis is an art-direction brief,
not a claim that the current system can already match their production quality.

## Review requirements

Before calling a visual pass polished, inspect front, side, and three-quarter
views; compare neutral and modified proportions; inspect hairlines, eyelids,
garment edges, folds, skin/material transitions, and feet-to-ground contact.
Check the exported GLB as well as the viewport. Keep unresolved quality gaps
visible in the project notes rather than hiding them behind feature counts.

Do not publish the existing mannequin/surface-shell presentation as the finished
quality target. Existing controls and infrastructure remain useful, but the next
work must improve the rendered model and garment construction.

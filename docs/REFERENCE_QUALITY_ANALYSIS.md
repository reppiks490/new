# Reference quality analysis — second image batch

Recorded 2026-10-08. Numbers follow the eight images in the user's message,
top to bottom. These notes analyze visible art and rendering. The images
themselves are not copied into the repository. This is project-local memory.

## Evidence limits

These small, compressed final images show appearance, not topology, UVs, rig
weights, source resolution, shaders, renderer choice, or animation behavior.
Do not infer polygon counts, texture sizes, true subsurface scattering, cloth
simulation, or manufacturability from a flattering still image.

The set contains different styles and quality levels: collectible-style sculpt
presentation, stylized game rendering, and more photographic surface treatment.
Separate asset quality, deformation, materials, and image presentation when
evaluating progress. Resolution or slider count does not establish art quality.

## 1 — Raised-leg figure on a display base

The strongest qualities are sculpt readability and accessory construction. The
action silhouette is clear, the standing leg and stone base anchor the pose,
and large muscle forms remain legible despite substantial compression at the
bent joints. Boots show layered construction, laces, creases, and thickness.
Fabric, gloves, hair, skin, boots, and the base read as different materials.
The simple dark background directs attention to the object.

Broad, glossy highlights also make parts read like painted resin. That suits a
collectible aesthetic but is not the best universal skin benchmark. One carefully
chosen pose does not establish deformation quality through motion. STL branding
does not prove watertight geometry or printable wall thickness.

Use this reference for finished sculpting, pose correctives, constructed
accessories, contact with the ground, and turntable presentation. Inspect extreme
joint bends from multiple angles before claiming the rig supports them.

## 2 — Green costume, raised arms, urban background

The face has intentional stylization, readable eyes, and controlled highlights.
Braids form a coherent hair design rather than arbitrary strands. Skin and fabric
respond differently to the light, while the blurred geometric background provides
context without taking over the composition. The overall color palette is clear.

The surface is very smooth in the available image. It does not demonstrate pores
or broad cloth-simulation capability; the garment remains close-fitting. The low
camera and perspective strongly affect apparent proportions. Neutral views are
needed to judge underlying shape rather than camera exaggeration.

Use it for identity preservation, hair organization, garment edges, and final
portrait framing. Keep separate neutral inspection and presentation cameras.

## 3 — Three figures in a rear three-quarter composition

This is useful for silhouette and shape transitions. Torso, hip, and upper-leg
forms connect smoothly, and the three figures retain distinct identities through
hair, faces, proportions, and clothing. Overlap and darker contact regions convey
depth. Clothing trim, jacket graphics, gloves, and skin have distinct visual roles.

The overlapping poses hide some contact areas, so the image is not proof of
collision-free meshes. The resolution is insufficient to judge fine textures or
topology. Smooth contouring in a still does not guarantee that the same shapes
remain stable under a different pose or morph combination.

Use it for rear/three-quarter inspection, coherent regional transitions,
multi-character contact checks, and preset individuality. Avoid merely widening
the same region without reshaping neighboring contours.

## 4 — Cool, moody studio portrait

The more photographic impression comes from a combination of narrow highlights,
soft shadow gradients, localized reflectivity, and restrained background color.
The face and loose hair elements receive deliberate lighting. Surface variation
is more convincing than one uniform gloss value across the entire character.

The dramatic lighting hides some contours and surface transitions. Reflective
detail can obscure form as well as enhance it. The image cannot establish whether
the material remains convincing under neutral, bright, or backlit conditions.

Use it for separate color, roughness, and fine surface-detail layers, controlled
specular highlights, and warm/cool lighting tests. Do not describe uniform shine
as realistic skin, or claim physical scattering without implementing it.

## 5 — Low-angle industrial-setting portrait

The craft is in material separation and garment construction: fabric panels,
straps, fittings, painted markings, and skin catch the light differently. Strong
directional light defines large forms. Background beams reinforce spatial depth,
and the camera creates a deliberate, dramatic presentation.

Perspective amplifies nearby forms. Dark regions hide detail, and high contrast
can make a rough asset look more finished. Thin straps and hardware need explicit
clearance, anchoring, and pose checks; their appearance in one view is insufficient.

Use it to require actual panels, edges, seams, and hardware instead of body paint.
Judge the result under flatter diagnostic light as well as the dramatic preset.

## 6 — Layered clothing and accessories in a two-character composition

Denim seams and edges, folded clothing, reflective hardware, and layered
accessories create multiple scales of visual information. Hair has readable
larger masses with finer breakup. Faces communicate distinct expressions and
directions of attention. Dense detail is organized into distinguishable materials.

Visual complexity does not establish correct deformation. The tight crop and
overlap obscure contact areas. Fine textures cannot compensate for incorrect
silhouette, stretched joints, or intersecting clothing.

Carry forward the non-explicit craft qualities: layered garment assembly,
expression targets, material-specific responses, and actual contact checks.
Do not reproduce the sexual scene or actions.

## 7 — Two figures against a dark studio background

The uncluttered composition is a useful comparison of character silhouettes and
large muscle forms. Distinct faces and accessories separate the identities.
Broad highlights reveal volume without a detailed environment.

Compared with other references, skin and hair appear smoother and more uniform,
with a stronger game-render aesthetic and less visible surface variation. Some
transitions are heavily stylized. This is a useful shape/identity reference,
but not the strongest material benchmark in the set.

Use it for consistent preset comparisons, coherent muscle/fat distribution,
and a simple inspection environment. Do not hide shape defects behind scenery.

## 8 — Close portrait with braids and soft lighting

Gentle gradients reveal facial planes without harsh contrast. Eye appearance,
lip color, cheek color, brows, and asymmetry contribute to a finished face.
The braids have a readable woven structure, with highlights aligned to hair flow.
A close camera raises the quality requirement for lids, hairlines, and facial
transitions beyond what a distant full-body viewport can demonstrate.

Subtle asymmetry could be intentional or an artifact; this still does not prove
facial-rig quality. Braids are organized into substantial clumps, so fine-strand
quality should not be assumed. The crop says little about the rest of the asset.

Use it for portrait inspection, authored hair/groom structure, facial material
variation, eyelid/eye integration, and separation of identity from expression.

## Resulting quality priorities

1. Deliberate underlying sculpt: facial identity, silhouette, and connected forms.
2. Morphs that preserve that finish. Breast and glute size remain priority
   controls, but transitions, contour, asymmetry, and garment response matter.
3. Garments with thickness, edges, seams, trim, folds, and meaningful clearances.
4. Hair/groom assets and facial finishing. A bald single-material base cannot
   reach these references through a larger polygon count alone.
5. Separately tuned skin, fabric, leather, hair, and metal materials, combined
   with coherent light and grounded shadows.
6. Before/after comparisons with the same camera and lighting, followed by
   front, side, back, portrait, and modified-shape checks, including exports.

The browser currently remains a technical prototype. These references define
the review standard; they do not establish that the existing implementation
already meets it. Keep remaining gaps explicit.
